Rung Rings: Privacy Policy

Effective date: 5 November 2026 (applies to version 3.4 and later; earlier versions collected no data at all)

Rung Rings is a quiet puzzle game. From version 3.4 it shows short ads from Google and counts installs with Google's Firebase Analytics, and those two Google services receive some data from your device. Our own code collects nothing and sends nothing. This page explains, in plain language, what that means in practice.

The short version

  • No accounts. You never sign up or log in.
  • No tracking prompt, and no tracking: the game never asks to track you and never reads your device's advertising identifier.
  • Short ads between levels, from the second world on, served by Google AdMob. They are not personalised and are rated for general audiences. If you played before version 3.4, they stay off for you.
  • Google receives the data its ad and analytics services need (listed below). We, the developer, never receive anything that identifies you.
  • Your progress stays on your device, and optionally in your own iCloud.

Advertising and purchases

Ads. From the second world, a short ad can play between levels, only after you tap Next. They are served by Google AdMob. Every ad request asks for non-personalised ads, with the maximum ad content rating G, so ads are not chosen from a profile of you. To show and count ads, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects, as Google describes it: your IP address (used to estimate a general location), an app- or developer-bounded device identifier (not the advertising identifier, which the game never reads), the ads you have seen and how you interacted with them, and crash and performance data. Google's own policy explains what Google does with it: https://policies.google.com/privacy and https://policies.google.com/technologies/ads.

A spool for a short ad. In the hint tray you can choose to watch a short ad for one spool. It only plays when you ask.

Consent. In the European Economic Area, the UK and Switzerland, Google's consent form appears once, at a menu, before any ad, and you can change your answer any time in Settings > Privacy options.

US state privacy rights. If you live in a US state with a consumer privacy law (for example California), Settings > Privacy options lets you opt out of the "sale" or "sharing" of personal information, as those laws define them, through Google's form. The game itself sells nothing.

Install counting (Firebase Analytics)

The game uses Google's Firebase Analytics, without advertising-identifier support, so that ad campaigns can count how many people install the game. It records the events Firebase collects automatically (the first open, sessions, app updates) with an app instance identifier, and a general location estimated from your IP address. The game sends no events of its own, no user ID and no screen names, and has ad personalisation signals turned off. Google describes this data at https://support.google.com/analytics/answer/10285841.

What the app stores, and where

Your progress. Which levels you have finished, your stars and points, which tutorials and story pages you have seen, and your Settings choices. This is saved in a small file on your device, inside the app's own storage. Deleting the app deletes it. "Reset progress" in Settings clears it (the ads-off flag of early players stays).

iCloud sync (optional, handled by Apple). If you are signed in to iCloud on your device, the app also saves a copy of your progress in iCloud's key-value store, so that it can follow you to a new phone or restore after a reinstall. That copy lives on Apple's servers, under your own Apple Account. It is private to you. We, the developer, cannot read it, cannot see it and never receive it. If you are signed out of iCloud, the game works the same and keeps progress on the device only. Apple's handling of iCloud data is described in Apple's own privacy policy.

A local play log. The app writes a short text log of game events (for example "level 4 solved in 5 moves", or "hint used") to a file in the app's own folder on your device. It contains no name, no account, no device identifier and no free text. It is never uploaded and nothing in the app sends it anywhere. Deleting the app deletes the file.

What we never do

  • We do not collect personal data ourselves, and we have no server.
  • We do not track you across other apps or websites, and the game never reads the advertising identifier.
  • We do not sell, rent or share data, because we do not have any.
  • We do not ask for permissions such as location, camera, microphone, contacts or photos.

Links that open outside the app

Settings contains links to this privacy policy and to the support page. They open in your browser (Safari), outside the game. These pages are static web pages hosted on GitHub Pages. Like any website, the host may keep ordinary server logs of visits (such as your IP address) when you open them; that is governed by the host's own policy.

Information Apple may provide

If you have chosen to share crash reports and analytics with app developers in iOS Settings, or you install the game through TestFlight, Apple may make anonymised crash reports and usage statistics available to the developer. This is Apple's system, controlled by you in iOS Settings.

Children

Rung Rings is a general-audience game. It has no accounts, no chat and no personal data collection of its own. Ads are rated G (for general audiences) and are never personalised, and the game is not directed at children. We do not knowingly collect information from anyone, including children.

Changes to this policy

If the app ever changes in a way that affects your privacy, this page will be updated first, the effective date above will change, and the App Store privacy details will be updated to match.
